My ordering assistant can now take a complete burger order from start to finish, and there still isn't a real language model in it.

That's deliberate. The LLM's only job will be to turn a message into typed actions. Everything else is deterministic: the cart, the next question, the order lifecycle, the replies. So I built all of it first, against a fake model.

The nice part: "2 sencillas, una sin cebolla, y una coca grande" becomes two distinct lines plus a drink, and the tests run in about a second with no network.

The humbling part: when I reviewed my own code, I found a customer could cancel a confirmed order and be told it worked, while the kitchen kept cooking. I also found that "sí" to "anything else?" was ending the order instead of continuing it. Short answers only mean something in context, and that's where the bugs were.

Result: 435 green tests, 68 of them end-to-end across two restaurant configs, and 5 of 9 turns in a sample order needed no model at all.

Next up: PostgreSQL, so a restart doesn't lose an order.
